# Permanent G6 scientific charter

AI Measurement Equity for Socially Vulnerable Communities studies how AI measurements represent the physical environments of NYC neighborhoods. All eight reference surfaces are outcomes. No single surface or hazard governs the paper. Intersectional measurement equity (RQ2) is central. Use the same eligible tracts, jointly observed areas and published map for validity, error disparities and inferential consequences. Independent ACS/SVI measurements describe places, not individuals. Disagreement with a map partly derived from the input imagery does not establish field truth.

1. RQ1: Estimate agreement and stability for each native surface and defensible domain, with pixel confusion only for eligible segmentation outputs and tract share error for all compatible families.
2. RQ2 (primary): Estimate differences in signed error, absolute error and coverage across intersecting poverty, racialized residential composition, age and housing conditions. Assess reference composition, common support, spatial dependence and ACS uncertainty.
3. RQ3: Estimate model-induced distortion of separate inequality contrasts in percentage points, and omissions and additions under a hypothetical joint environmental improvement rule. No individual exposure, universal vulnerability score or actual allocation is identified.

Before each experiment record construct, unit, reference, independent social source, common support, spatial separation, downstream estimand and decision that the result could change. Preserve null and reversed findings. Use the existing Featurize environment for raster and model operations; retain frozen historical records.
